Context:
  This repository's bounded odd frontier for the holdout {12,16,20} reached
  409 (RUN_12_16_20_ODD*.txt) without finding a good odd reply, and Blok's
  report "Sylver Coinage positions with g=2" (2021) closes with: "the
  completion requires knowing a good, odd reply to {12,16,20}, which so
  far, I do not know."  The same report proves, by the simple pairing
  strategy, that {8,12} is a P-position.  Both the report and this
  repository overlooked that the even reply 8 settles the holdout.

Theorem ({8,12} is P; Blok 2021, made proof-grade here):
  The gaps of <8,12> are 1, 4, every odd number, and every number 2 mod 4.
  Pair them as 2<->3, 4<->6, (4j+1,4j+3) for j>=1, and (8j+2,8j+6) for
  j>=1, with 1 unpaired.  For every finite set C of completed pairs, the
  configuration monoid T_C generated by {8,12} and the members of C
  satisfies:

  1. Gap structure: gaps(T_C) = {1} union whole pairs.  With a the least
     completed odd index, alpha marking whether pair a+1 is completed, b
     the least completed even index, and sigma1/sigma2 the special pairs:
     sigma1 leaves gaps {1}; otherwise the odd gaps are the odd numbers
     below 4a+1 plus {4a+5,4a+7} when neither sigma2 nor alpha holds; the
     2-mod-4 gaps are {2} under sigma2 and otherwise {2,6} plus
     {8j+2,8j+6 : j < min(a,b)}; and 4 is a gap exactly when sigma2 fails.
  2. Mate survival: if {x,y} is a whole pair of gaps(T_C) and x is played,
     y = t + k*x would need k=0 (y not a gap), k>=2 (t<0 since 2x>y), or
     k=1 with t=|y-x| in {1,2,4}; 1 is never in T_C, 2 requires sigma1
     (no pairs left), and 4 requires sigma2 (no difference-four pair left).
  3. Termination: completing {2,3} leaves {1}; even-pair completions
     strictly decrease min(a,b); the first odd or special completion
     leaves finitely many gaps.  So the mate strategy never names 1 and
     the opponent eventually must.

Corollary (new here):
  8 is a gap of <12,16,20>, and 16=8+8, 20=8+12 give the exact identity

    <12,16,20,8> = <8,12>.

  Hence {12,16,20} is an N-position with the even winning reply 8, and the
  gcd-four candidate branch <16,20,28,12> = <12,16,20> (28=12+16) is
  refuted by the same reply.  No odd tail theorem is needed: the family
  formula F(<12,16,20,m>)=3m+8 and the 409-deep odd frontier searched the
  side of the position that cannot settle it.
