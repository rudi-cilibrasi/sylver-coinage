Theorem:
  For every odd integer m >= 3,

    <12,16,20> = {0} union {4*t : t >= 3}.

  Four copies of m belong to this even subsemigroup, so every coefficient of
  m reduces uniquely to k in {0,1,2,3}.  In residue class k*m modulo four:

    - every positive integer below k*m is a gap;
    - k*m is represented;
    - k*m+4 and k*m+8 are gaps; and
    - k*m+4*t is represented for every t >= 3.

  These four residue classes partition the positive integers.  Therefore the
  complete gap set, not just its endpoint, is explicit and

    Frobenius(<12,16,20,m>) = 3*m+8,
    genus(<12,16,20,m>) = (3*m+13)/2.

  Since the Frobenius number is odd, symmetry would require genus
  (F+1)/2.  The displayed genus is instead (F+5)/2, a constant defect of two;
  pseudo-symmetry requires even F.  Thus the standard symmetric-or-pseudo-
  symmetric sufficient condition for an N-position cannot settle this tail.

  The same residue description leaves exactly two pseudo-Frobenius numbers,
  3*m+4 and 3*m+8.  They are both above F/2 and hence are precisely the two
  end moves of the Sylver position.  The standard one-ender strategy-stealing
  theorem therefore cannot settle any member of the family either.

Strategy-stealing bottleneck:
  Set F=3*m+8 and provisionally play F.  A direct calculation in the four
  gap classes proves that every other legal move makes F representable except

    2*m+4 and 3*m+4.

  Below-anchor gaps x satisfy F-x=(3-j)*m+(8+j*m-x), with the second term an
  even-semigroup element at least 12.  Top gaps j*m+8 eliminate F in one
  copy.  For the j*m+4 gaps, two copies handle 4 and m+4; the displayed two
  exceptions are the remaining j=2,3 cases.  Thus the ordinary strategy-
  stealing probe proves the child N unless every winning response after F is
  confined to those two moves.  The finite frontier has a nonexceptional
  winning response in every row, but no unbounded proof of that fact is known.
