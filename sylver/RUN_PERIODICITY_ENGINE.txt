Finite-state reduction implemented:
  Let E be an even oversemigroup of the starting position, let f be the
  Frobenius number after division by two, and put tbar=2f.  A gcd-one child is
  represented by E and its live odd anchors.

  * An odd move below min(anchor)-tbar resets every anchor and leaves a
    single-anchor state.  Its outcome is represented by one monotone P-hit
    flag for E.
  * An odd move above max(anchor)+tbar is generated and illegal.
  * Live anchors span at most tbar.  Once their minimum exceeds tbar, two
    anchors sum above every even gap and three sum above the odd reply
    window, so the complete recurrence depends only on E and the anchor
    offsets.
  * Even oversemigroups, anchor-offset shapes, all outcome rows in the last
    tbar window, and stable reset flags form a finite state.  Exact equality
    of two states certifies repetition forever.

  sylver/periodicity.py is the executable specification.  The C++ engine is
  the campaign implementation: transitions are materialized lazily in game
  order, every new even part reconstructs its complete earlier singleton
  history, finite fallbacks select the smallest sufficient 64-bit word count,
  and completed fallbacks are checkpointed immediately.  Period detection
  uses exact bit-packed snapshots with Brent's algorithm, not a hash match.
  Snapshots are rejected while any reset flag is transient.  When external
  exact rows are loaded, cycle detection waits until their largest anchor and
  the entire tbar window have passed, preventing an incomplete-oracle cycle.

Scope:
  This is a proof-grade implementation advance and an exact extension of the
  dependency frontier, not a solution of the bounty problem.  A P-HIT for the
  base row would prove X N.  Otherwise only an exact repeated full snapshot
  after all external-cache anchors have left the window can prove the odd
  tail contains no P child.  No finite-prefix extrapolation is permitted.
